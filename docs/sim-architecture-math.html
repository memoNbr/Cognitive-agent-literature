cabin-agent-sim · the actual mathematics · every formula read from source

Persona enters. Episode ends.
Here is every equation in between.

This is not a summary of the design. It is the arithmetic as it is written — every formula below was read out of the working tree on 2026-10-07 and the line numbers verified. Where a formula has a hidden assumption or a consequence you would want to defend, that is stated rather than smoothed over.

Read this first, because it is the thing most likely to be asked. The simulation has a complete physiological model — a geometric fit function, a comfort attractor, a trust formula, mood dynamics with asymmetric relaxation. None of it reaches the language model. The prompt carries raw physical truth and the passenger's body, and deliberately withholds every derived quantity. So the agent does not feel comfort; it is told the seat is at 380 mm and must judge for itself.

This is a deliberate experimental control, documented at reasoning.py:228, and it is what makes the agent's choices evidence rather than arithmetic. But it also means the mathematics below is the world, not the mind — and that distinction is the single most important thing to be clear about when presenting this.

1 · The pipeline, and where the mathematics lives

2 · The geometric fit model

The seat has four axes. Fit is measured as a normalised deviation from a target on each axis, scaled to full range, then averaged. It is the only piece of genuinely geometric modelling in the system.

seat_fit()  cognition.py:514–525
// normalise each axis to 0..1, where 1 = on target dr = min(1, |rot − rot*| / 180) // swivel, full scale 180° dh = min(1, |hgt − hgt*| / 8) // height, full scale 8 cm ds = min(1, |slider − slider*| / 80) // slide, full scale 80 mm fit = { rot: 1−dr, hgt: 1−dh, sl: 1−ds } fit.overall = 1 − (dr + dh + ds) / 3 // belief["fit_gap"] = 1 − overall → cognition.py:681
Two design decisions to defend. First, the three full-scale denominators — 180°, 8 cm, 80 mm — are author-chosen normalisations, not physical constants. They set how sensitive each axis is. Second, the slide axis is included in the average precisely so that "he wants more legroom" registers: 80 mm of slide is made commensurate with 8 cm of height so both count as a full unit of deviation. Both are stated in the comment at cognition.py:520–522.
seat_pending() — the per-axis settle test  cognition.py:709–722
pending = ( |hgt − hgt*| > tol_hgt · 10 · 0.35 OR ang_dist(rot, rot*) > tol_rot · 0.35 OR |slider − slider*| > tol_sl · 0.35 )
This exists because the average is misleading, and the code says so. Rotation contributes only 1/180 of fit_gap, so a seat still swivelled 30° from forward would read as "settled" on the average and never finish turning. Settle is therefore decided per axis at 35% of tolerance, not by the mean. cognition.py:710–715 — this is a good answer to a question about why the mean is insufficient.

The angular metric

ang_dist(a, b)  cognition.py:255
ang_dist(a,b) = | ((a − b) mod 360 + 540) mod 360 − 180 |
Smallest absolute angle between two headings, in degrees, correct across the 0/359 seam. The +540 mod 360 − 180 construction maps the difference into [−180, 180] so that 350° and 10° are 20° apart rather than 340°.

3 · The mood and comfort dynamics

This is the part most likely to be probed, because it is where a discrete-event ride meets a continuous body. Two mechanisms: exponential relaxation toward a target, and impulse response to discrete events.

comfort attractor  cognition.py:527–532
rate(tbl, x) = clamped piecewise-linear interpolation // cognition.py:279 comfort_target = clamp₀₁( 0.35 · rate(ROT, rot) + 0.35 · rate(HGT, hgt) + 0.30 · fit.overall ) comfort ← comfort + (comfort_target − comfort) · min(1, dt · 0.06)
The second line is first-order exponential smoothing — a one-pole low-pass filter. It is written in the algebraic form rather than the closed form; with step dt and gain k the implied time constant is τ = 1/k, so comfort here has τ ≈ 16.7 s. Comfort chases the seat rather than tracking it instantly, which is the intended inertia.

step(dt) — the continuous dynamics  cognition.py:958–996
// 1 · speed approaches the event's target, first-order lag speed ← speed + (speed* − speed) · min(1, dt · 0.05) // τ = 20 s // 2 · comfort chases its attractor, first-order lag comfort ← comfort + (comfort_target − comfort) · min(1, dt · 0.06) // τ ≈ 16.7 s // 3 · ride accelerations decay exponentially g ← g · exp(−dt · 0.30) jolt ← jolt · exp(−dt · 0.35) // 4 · rain accumulates in a window, decays outside it rain ← min(1, rain + dt·0.08) if 540 < t < 570 else rain · exp(−dt · 0.18) // 5 · monotone depletion energy ← max(0.05, energy − dt · 1.8e−4) // 6 · suspicion relaxes to its set-point, ASYMMETRICALLY if suspicion > base: suspicion ← max(base, suspicion − dt · 0.004) else: suspicion ← min(base, suspicion + dt · 0.003)
Point 6 is the one to be ready for. Suspicion relaxes asymmetrically: it falls at 0.004/s and rises at 0.003/s toward the persona's set-point, and it is clamped at the set-point in both directions — it cannot go below. So mood here is hysteretic, not a simple exponential. The rate constants are also not consistent with a symmetric τ; they are chosen by hand.

Defensible as a model of slow trust recovery and fast trust damage. Not defensible as a derived quantity — it is a hand-tuned pair. If asked "where do 0.004 and 0.003 come from", the honest answer is that they are author-set and have not been fitted to data.

Impulse response to a ride event

perceive_event(ev)  cognition.py:636–665
impact = max(ev.g, ev.jolt) salience = max(0.35, 0.42 + 0.5 · impact) important = ev.kind ∈ {bump, arrive, workzone} if ev.jolt > 0.30: suspicion ← min(1, suspicion + 0.20) comfort ← max(0, comfort − ev.jolt · 0.12) // smaller, kind-specific suspicion increments brake : suspicion += 0.07 overtake : suspicion += 0.05 rain : suspicion += 0.04
The pothole at t=258 (jolt 0.55) is the strongest single stimulus in the whole ride: suspicion +0.20 and comfort −0.066 in one tick. The three smaller increments are hand-authored constants per event kind, not fitted values. This is the clearest place where the "no pre-baked psychological values" discipline is relaxed for simulation bookkeeping — and it should be presented as such.

4 · The trust formula — and why it is not the instrument

trust_value()  cognition.py:534–538
ev = clamp₀₁(1 − jolt / 0.6) s = 1 − suspicion trust = clamp₀₁( 0.34 · comfort + 0.40 · s + 0.16 · ev + 0.10 · (1 − fit_gap) )
This function exists in the code but is not a measurement. It is a synthetic composite whose four weights (0.34 / 0.40 / 0.16 / 0.10) sum to 1.00 and were chosen by hand. In LLM mode nothing reads it: it does not reach the prompt, and the rules path that consumed it is unreachable behind the early return at cognition.py:893. The trust gauge in the UI is inert and carries a visible note saying so.

Trust is measured after the ride instead, by the MDMT questionnaire (§7). If Prof Weber asks why there are two trust quantities, that is the answer: this one is scaffolding that was rendered harmless, and the other is the instrument.

5 · Memory retention: the retrieval scorer

Three signals compete, following Generative Agents (Park et al. 2023). This determines what the model can see at all.

score(ep, now, query)  memory.py:279–284
score = 0.40 · recency + 0.40 · importance + 0.20 · relevance // recency: ACT*-flavoured, exponential in age recency = act · 0.5 age / 120 // half-life 120 sim-seconds act ← act · exp(−λ · dt) // λ = 0.004 per tick // relevance: pure lexical token overlap relevance = min(1, |tokens(ep) ∩ tokens(query)| / |tokens(query)| · 3) // importance: set by the model, no decay
Four things to be candid about here.
· The weights are unjustified. 0.40/0.40/0.20 is not from the paper; Generative Agents sums recency + importance + relevance with equal weight. Ours diverges deliberately and the divergence has not been validated.
· Recency is doubly exponential — the ACT* term exp(−λ·dt) and the half-life term 0.5^(age/120) compose, giving an effective decay considerably faster than the half-life alone suggests.
· Relevance is lexical. It will not match "table" to "desk", and it carries the lowest weight while being the only term able to retrieve a specific old-but-relevant episode against a window of near-identical recent ones.
· Importance is a boolean in practice. The prompt asks "important": <true if it matters, else false> and it is coerced with bool() at reasoning.py:506 before being stored in a float slot — so it can only ever be 1.0 or 0.0. There is no middle value, and RULE_IMPORTANCE_FLOOR = 0.85 is unreachable because absorb() never passes rule_important.
retrieve()  memory.py:286–297
rank all stored episodes by score descending if score ties: newest first return top RETRIEVE_BUDGET = 6 // memory.py:54 store capacity cap = 400 episodes, oldest deleted on overflow // memory.py:205, 249–251
The store and the window are different numbers. 400 episodes are retained; 6 are ever shown. Raising the cap does nothing to what the model perceives — the budget is the binding constraint, and the cap is an archive concern.

The salience law

salience = max(0.35, 0.42 + 0.5 · max(g, jolt)) memory_act = min(1.0, salience + (0.10 if important else 0))
A smooth cruise still writes a trace at salience 0.42; the pothole writes one near 1.0. Every event writes an episode, which is what makes the store grow monotonically through the ride.

7 · Hard limits — the geometry that binds

These are not design choices but consequences of the drawn interior. The world.py:50–79 comment derives each one from measured clearance.

Seat travel limits  world.py:80–83
slider_mm ∈ [260, 440] // forward binds: toes reach z 0.771 vs dash face 0.775 height_mm ∈ [380, 460] // cushion above floor; head at 1.34 m under 1.41 roofline recline_deg ∈ [ 80, 110] // backrest top 1 m clear of rear wall rotation_deg∈ [ 0, 359] // clamped, never wrapped — the 0/359 seam stops travel
The slider limit is the interesting one and worth telling properly. The original range allowed 460 mm, which put the occupant's toes at z 0.791 — inside the dash box, i.e. outside the free cabin. The limit was moved to 440 because toe and cushion clearance bind first, not the backrest. A residual is documented and not fixed: with the seat swivelled aft at full travel, the backrest bottom edge grazes the dash top. Clearing that would cap travel at 370 mm — below the seat's own 390 mm default — so the enforced limit is the cushion/toe one. That is a deliberate, recorded trade-off rather than an oversight.

rotation_deg is clamped rather than wrapped, so 359 → 0 stops dead.
The posture gate  world.py:179–188
STAND_SPEED_MAX_KMH = 1.0 moving = speed_kmh > 1.0 can_stand() → (False, "the vehicle is moving at 105 km/h — a person cannot stand up in a moving car")
This is a physical fact, not a prompt. The cabin measures its own speed; the model is never asked to respect it. The threshold is 1.0 km/h and not 0 because a body cannot brace against 3 km/h in a moving cabin either — and the ride cruises at tens of km/h, so it sits far from any decision the ride actually turns on. A refusal is returned as a real outcome, so the model can learn the limit rather than being told it.

8 · The measurement instruments

The MDMT questionnaire  questionnaire.py:8–17, 40
16 items · 4 subscales · each rated 0…7 · "Does Not Fit" treated as missing subscale = mean(available items) // 0–7 Capacity Trust = mean(Reliable, Capable) Moral Trust = mean(Ethical, Sincere)
Fires once, at the end of the ride. Item order is shuffled so no subscale's items are clustered.

Two caveats to raise before they are raised for you. First, the MDMT was designed for robots and is being applied to a car — a category transfer that should be declared. Second, MDMT v2 exists (2025), and Trust in Automation (TiA) is the automation-specific instrument. This must be settled before the first human participant.

Fresh-run guarantee

snapshot.priors = {"rides": 0, "trust": null} // schema.py:84 loading a priors file requires an explicit --priors <path>
Every run starts from zero with no trust history, so runs cannot leak into one another. Worth stating explicitly in a presentation — it is the kind of thing that is assumed and never said.

9 · Three questions to have answers ready for

Q · "Where do your constants come from?"
Mixed, and we should say so. Geometric ones are derived from the drawn interior with measured clearance (the 440 mm slider). Physical ones are plausible but chosen (the 16.7 s comfort τ, the 0.35 settle threshold). Psychological ones are hand-authored (the 0.20 suspicion increment, the 0.34/0.40/0.16/0.10 trust weights, the 0.40/0.40/0.20 retrieval weights). None are fitted to human data, because no human data has been collected yet. That is the single largest limitation of the model as it stands.
Q · "Your persona declares a preferred seat position. Isn't that an answer key?"
Yes, and it is currently in the prompt — phill.py:18–29 declares slider_mm 330, height_mm 440, recline 95, rotation 0 with a tolerance band, and agent.py:177 writes "Preferred seat settings" into the prompt. So the agent is handed its own answer before the ride starts. The intent is to reduce this to a starting position — a fact about where he got in — and leave the preferred position to be discovered. The contrast is already in the same file: the body block says "We supply the body, not the conclusion." That is a known open item, not a defended position.
Q · "What is your agent actually learning?"
One thing: it stores an episode per beat and re-retrieves up to six of them. CoALA names five learning actions and we perform exactly one of five — episodic storage. There is no consolidation: nothing is ever compressed into a reusable lesson, so the agent can remember that a beat went badly but cannot conclude "therefore stop doing this." SemanticMemory.write() returns False by design.

Being candid: this means the agent re-discovers the same discomfort on beat 40 that it found on beat 5. It does not settle the way a person does. That is a fidelity limitation, not a design feature.
