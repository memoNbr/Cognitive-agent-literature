A person gets in, and works the seat

Ten beats. A passenger sits down, finds the cushion too low, raises it in two steps, and discovers that the top of the rail is right — then sits there for the rest of the ride. This is the scenario your study needs: one parameter, discovered, then held.

4 · I was wrong about what would happen

I told you the budget of 6 would crowd the discovery out, because the agent repeats itself. So I ran it to the end of a full 600-second ride and watched the rank.

The crowding failure does not happen. I predicted the agent would fill the window with its own repetitions and push out the moment it found the good position. It does not — because that episode carries importance 0.85 against the routine beats' 0.15, and importance never decays. Recency falls to almost nothing (1.0 → 0.0005) and the episode stays at rank 4 for the entire ride.

So my prediction was wrong and the measurement is what you should plan against. I have removed the claim rather than softening it.
But this reveals a worse problem, and it is the one to worry about.

Look at the score column: at beat 900 the discovery scores 0.4402 and a routine "still 440" beat scores 0.5768. The discovery is ranked above the window and its total score is almost entirely its own importance term — recency is 0.0005, worth 0.0002 of the 0.44.

An episode the model can still see but can no longer weigh. It is present in the prompt as text, so the LLM may notice it — but the score that put it there is now meaningless, and a single routine beat outranks it by 0.13. Keep it at importance 0.20 next beat and it leaves the window entirely. Keep it at 0.85 and it is an accident that it survives.

The thing protecting the agent's memory is a number the model wrote down about one beat. That is not a mechanism. It is luck with a good-looking trace.
What this means for your proposal. Raising the budget will not fix the above — the discovery already survives. The fragile part is not the count of slots; it is that a single self-reported importance value is the only thing standing between the agent and forgetting its own discovery. That is a stronger argument for revisiting the weights than a budget increase is.

It also means the budget of 6 is not obviously too small for the seat alone — 6 held the discovery for a whole 600-second ride. Your instinct about needing more applies when the subjects multiply, not when time passes.

Note the asymmetry: your eight prompts are in full on every single beat, while memory is rationed to six fragments. The variable you are trying to test gets more consistent attention than the agent's own history does. That is a defensible choice — the prompts are the independent variable and must not vary — but it is worth knowing, because it means the seat prompts will dominate the model's context relative to anything it remembers.
